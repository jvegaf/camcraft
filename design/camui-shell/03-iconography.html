Campilot — iconography

One hand drew all of it. 24 × 24 box, 1.6 stroke, round caps and joins, no fill except a 22 % wash for “this region is kept”, and dashed 2 2 for anything virtual — a mirror axis, a pitch circle, a construction line. Icons inherit currentColor, so the theme decides the hue and the set never needs a light variant. Drawn from scratch for Campilot: same rules as the reference, none of its paths.

Colours, type, spacing and radii are the tokens in design/system.md — the same graphite surfaces and two signals as 01-shell-2d — and the sheet is laid out as one card per family, the way 02-stages lays out its five.

RULES Five constants, no exceptions if a shape needs a sixth rule, the icon is the problem

One weight

1.6 at 24 px, everywhere. No icon is drawn “bolder” to feel important.

Dashed = virtual

Hairline dashed 2 2. An axis you can mirror across, not a thing you can cut.

Wash = kept

Fill at 22 % marks the region that survives an operation. Dashed marks what goes.

Safe area

Nothing crosses 2 px from the edge; optical mass sits dead centre.

No letters

Not one alphabet glyph in the set. A letter inside an icon is a failure of drawing.

ANATOMY One icon, measured the same 24 grid every time

Geometry

Box 24 × 24 · stroke 1.6 · safe area 2…22 · terminal radius 0.8 (round cap) · smallest detail 3 px · corners on rectangles rx 1–2.

Every icon is authored on the same grid, so a rail of twenty reads as one family rather than twenty drawings. Two shapes maximum, three only when the third is a dot marking a node.

At real sizes

The rail renders at 20 px inside its 32 × 32 target; the app bar takes 18, and zoom-dependent overlays may need 16. All three are cut, not scaled down from a detailed drawing: no icon carries a detail that dies below 16 px.

In the rail

Active tool = cyan wash + 1 px cyan border around a 32 px target. The glyph keeps its 1.6 weight, so the rail never shimmers as the selection moves. Every icon-only control carries a tooltip with its name and its key.

COUNT 71 icons across six families

The last four — undo, redo, theme-light and theme-dark — were added when the app bar was built: the vocabulary has to cover the frame that uses it, or the shell invents a glyph at the call site. The other sixty-seven are the commands.

What the set refuses

· A filled version of the same icon for “active” — the wash does that, and it costs no drawing.

· Perspective, isometric views, or 3D shading inside a 24 px glyph — except the one cube, drawn on the same grid.

· Numbers, letters, or a badge anywhere.

· Tool marks in the icon that a real toolpath does not have (a “cut” icon that suggests a blade) — the icon names the operation, the canvas shows the geometry.

Implementation note for egui

The set is authored as path data and rasterised once at 18 px (plus 32 for dialogs) through resvg/usvg, then cached as textures tinted per theme. Nothing loads from disk at run time.

Each glyph is one <g> with stroke-linecap:round, stroke-linejoin:round and fill:none, so a single draw pass covers the whole rail. Dashes are pre-flattened into segments at raster time: egui’s stroker does not honour stroke-dasharray.
